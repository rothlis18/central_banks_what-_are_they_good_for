Central banks, tasked with price stability and full employment, operate as sovereign debt-monetization engines through exponential credit expansion and quantitative easing, eroding fiat purchasing power. The mathematical inevitability of this erosion is quantified by the exponential growth of monetary supply, which, when coupled with yield-curve control, directly devalues currency. The Cantillon effect, a wealth-transfer mechanism, injects newly created money into the hands of the wealthy, exacerbating income inequality. This process directly impacts household savings and real wage formation, as inflation, driven by institutional targeting (e.g., 2% annual inflation), erodes purchasing power. The systemic risk is evident in the structural friction between hard-asset capital preservation and inflation targeting, with centralized monetary policy posing a direct threat to economic autonomy.
