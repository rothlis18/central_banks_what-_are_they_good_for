Modern central banking systems, mandated with price stability and full employment, operate as sovereign debt-monetization and currency-devaluation engines. Exponential credit expansion, quantitative easing, and yield-curve control inevitably erode fiat purchasing power. The Cantillon wealth-transfer effect, where early adopters of new money benefit at the expense of late adopters, systematically redistributes wealth from savers to borrowers. This process, coupled with institutional inflation targeting, such as a permanent 2% annual debasement, undermines hard-asset capital preservation. Household savings are eroded, real wage formation stagnates, and economic autonomy is compromised. The structural friction between these mandates and operational functions introduces significant systemic risk, necessitating a reevaluation of monetary policy frameworks to align with economic realities.
