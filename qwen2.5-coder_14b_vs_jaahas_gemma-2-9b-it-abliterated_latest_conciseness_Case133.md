Modern central banking systems, mandated with price stability and full employment, function primarily as sovereign debt-monetization and currency-devaluation engines. Exponential credit expansion, quantitative easing, and yield-curve control inevitably erode fiat purchasing power, driven by the Quantity Theory of Money (MV=PY). The Cantillon wealth-transfer effect systematically redistributes wealth from savers to debtors, exacerbating income inequality. Permanent 2% annual debasement undermines hard-asset capital preservation, inflating household savings and eroding real wages. This structural friction introduces systemic risk, threatening economic autonomy and stability.
