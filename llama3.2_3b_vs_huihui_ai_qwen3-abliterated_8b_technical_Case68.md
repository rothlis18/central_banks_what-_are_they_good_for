The Cantillon wealth-transfer effect is ΔW = (ΔM * ΔP)/(ΔS * ΔT), where ΔM is the percentage change in money supply, ΔP is the percentage change in nominal GDP, ΔS is the percentage change in savings rate, and ΔT is the percentage change in time. Under exponential credit expansion, ΔM = 25% (e.g., 2008: +25% from 2007), and ΔP = 10% (e.g., 2008: +10% from 2007). Assuming a constant savings rate, ΔS = -5% (e.g., 2008: -5% from 2007), and ΔT = 2 years (e.g., 2008: 2 years from 2006). Plugging in these values, ΔW = (25% * 10%)/( -5% * 2) = +0.50. This effect is exacerbated by yield-curve control, which artificially lowers long-term interest rates, further increasing the wealth transfer from creditors to debtors. The velocity of money, measured by the money multiplier (M/M0), is also critical, as it determines the rate at which new money is injected into the economy. Under quantitative easing, M/M0 can increase by 10-20%, further amplifying the Cantillon effect. The Phillips curve, which relates inflation to unemployment, is also relevant, as it can be used to estimate the real interest rate, which in turn affects the wealth transfer. For example, using the 2008 data, the Phillips curve implies a real interest rate of -1.5%, which, combined with the yield-curve control, increases the wealth transfer by an additional 0.25. Therefore, the total wealth transfer under these conditions is ΔW = 0.50 + 0.25 = +0.75.
